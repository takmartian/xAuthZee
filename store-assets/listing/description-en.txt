[Long description]

XAuthZee is a Time-based One-Time Password (TOTP / RFC 6238) authenticator that lives inside your browser. It is small, fast, and respects your privacy — all your accounts are stored locally and synced through your browser's own sync infrastructure, never sent to any server we operate.


Why XAuthZee

Most authenticator apps live on a separate device, which means switching windows to grab a code. XAuthZee puts the codes where you already are — the browser tab that is asking for them.


Features

Scan any QR on the page. Click the scan button, drag a rectangle over the QR code. XAuthZee reads it, parses the otpauth:// URL, and saves the account.

One-click fill. Focus a one-time-code input on any site and XAuthZee shows a small panel with the matching code. Pick it and the code is typed into the page (or copied to the clipboard as a fallback when the site blocks scripting).

Host bindings. Bind accounts to specific hostnames so that when you visit, say, github.com, the right code is at the top of the list.

Drag to reorder. Grab the handle on the left of a row to rearrange your codes; the order syncs to your other devices. Codes matched by a binding on the current site stay pinned at the top and never take part in reordering.

Master password (optional). Encrypt your secret keys with a password you choose. The derived key lives in session storage and is cleared when the browser closes. The auto-lock timer (5/15/30/60 minutes or never) and the system's own lock screen both lock the extension immediately.

Import / export. Move accounts between devices as JSON. You can also paste a otpauth-migration:// link (Google Authenticator's transfer format) or its QR screenshot.

Cross-device sync. Because accounts are stored in chrome.storage.sync, they show up on every other Chrome signed in to the same Google account.

i18n. English and Simplified Chinese UI; the extension follows the browser's language.


Permissions, briefly

XAuthZee asks for the minimum set of permissions Chrome requires and nothing more.

storage — to save your accounts and preferences locally and sync them through your browser.

activeTab — only when you click the toolbar or scan button, to read the current tab's URL and (if you ask) capture the QR.

scripting — to inject the QR region-selector overlay when you ask to scan.

alarms — to schedule the auto-lock timer.

idle — to lock the extension when the operating system locks.

No analytics, no telemetry, no remote code, no third-party SDKs.


Open source

Source code and build instructions are available in the project repository. You can audit exactly what the extension does.


FAQ

Q: Where are my accounts stored?

A: Locally in your browser. If you sign in to the same Google account, Chrome syncs them to your other devices using its own encrypted sync. The developer never sees them.

Q: Does it support HOTP?

A: No — only TOTP (RFC 6238). HOTP is rarely used today and is a different security model.

Q: Can I import from Google Authenticator?

A: Yes. Open the GA transfer-export QR, copy the image, and paste (Cmd+V) it into the XAuthZee import dialog. Or copy the otpauth-migration:// URL string and paste that.

Q: What if I forget my master password?

A: Reset the extension: open options → security → turn off encryption with the password. If you cannot remember the password, the encrypted secrets are unrecoverable by design — re-add the accounts from their original QR codes.
